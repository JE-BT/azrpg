# The narrative: what story azrpg tells, and how

**Status: built in Alpha 5.1**, with the decisions in section 13. This document set out the story
layer of azrpg before it was built: how a story is
structured, how it is made from the map, and how it reaches the player. It answers the Alpha 5
playtest and the notes in the design document, and it is meant to be argued with. The mechanics come
after it is agreed.

Examples use Bernoy, whose twelve journeys are reported in [STORIES_BERNOY.md](STORIES_BERNOY.md).
Facts from the map are marked *(data)*.

---

## 1. Hidden machinery, visible world

The player meets people, places, prices, weather, news and the company. The story is what moves them,
and it stays out of sight. No hook, beat, shape or layer name ever reaches the screen.

| The player sees | Underneath |
|---|---|
| "You are Gukrul of Leg. Your sister Mazog rows with the Keels, and you go with her." | the **premise**: who you are, and why you, personally, are going |
| "But first: find a fence in Anholdhol who will take Scorc silver." | the current **leg role** and its objective |
| The raiders want to put in at a fishing village; a priest in Zin asks what you worship; salt doubles in price | **moments**: hooks in the map, carried by people and systems |
| A shrine-keeper who will not bless you; a watch that looks away when you are robbed | **identity**: how this person, here, reacts to who you are |
| "The haul so far: 🟡 31 and nine bales of wool." | the arc's **measure** |

The journal keeps the premise, the objective, the measure, the people you have met and the record of
what happened. It never lists beats.

## 2. The stack

A story is five simple systems, each with one job. None of them knows the inside of the others.

1. **Premise.** Who you are and why you go. Set once, at the start.
2. **Arc.** What this kind of journey wants: a goal, a measure that grows, and endings. One per
   journey type.
3. **Structure.** How the arc is paced: three acts, the hero's journey and the rest. Chosen for each
   journey at creation, by weighted chance.
4. **Leg roles.** What each leg is for: find the map, harry the coast, the false lead, the strike.
   Drawn from the arc's library, ordered by the structure, placed by the map.
5. **Moments.** What happens on the way: people, places, prices and events, drawn from the map's hooks,
   your identity and the state of the world.

The rest of this document takes them in turn.

## 3. Premise

**Fields.**

| Field | From | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | the traveller kind, people, faith, home and traits (data, Alpha 2) | Gukrul, an Uruk raider of Leg, of the Old Uruk Beliefs |
| Tie | the journey type, chosen by weighted chance from what the data allows | kin: a sister in the company |
| Stake | the arc's measure, personally | a raider's share of the haul |
| Standing | the kind and the tie | a new hand; a veteran; the leader |

**Ties.** A tie connects the traveller to the journey, and almost always introduces a character who
goes with them, waits for them, or sent them. Each type offers three or four; data rules some in or out.

| Tie | Reads | Who it introduces | Allowed when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kin | "Your [sister] goes, and you go with her." | a named companion, one of the company | the type has a company |
| Contract | "You signed on in [origin] for a share." | the recruiter, who keeps the books | the origin is a market town or port |
| Custom | "Among the [people], everyone of age goes." | the company's eldest | the traveller's culture is Naval or Nomadic (raids), or of the destination's faith (pilgrimages) |
| Faith | "You made a vow at [temple]." | the priest who heard it | the traveller keeps the journey's faith |
| Debt | "You owe [name] a sum you cannot pay. This pays it." | the creditor, at home | any |
| Duty | "[Ruler or temple] sent you." | the patron | power and faith families |
| Flight | "You cannot go back to [home]." | whoever you fled | exile and refugee |
| Curiosity | "You have read of [place] since you were a child." | a mentor, or nobody | expedition, wandering, treasure |

**The opening.** One card, then the game.

> You are **[name]**, [kind] of [home], [people] by birth and [faith] by faith. [Tie.] [A line of
> backstory from the kind.] You stand in **[place]**, [season and weather]. [What the company is doing
> on the first leg, and why.] [Lead or follow.] **But first: [the first objective].**

**Lead or follow.** For types that allow it, the opening asks. A leader holds the stores, decides the
stops, and is asked by the company ("the raiders want to put in at a village"). A follower is told
where the company goes, keeps a voice in the vote, and has stakes of their own.

## 4. Arcs: what each journey wants

Every arc has a **goal**, a **measure** that grows on the way and is spent or judged at the end, a
library of **leg roles**, and **endings**. The measure is what the player watches; endings read it.

| Type | Goal | Measure (what grows) | Endings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilgrimage | reach the seat of the faith, and be changed by it | devotion: rites kept, alms given, pilgrims met; a relic | raised in the faith; a relic carried home; sent out as a missionary; turned away; the vow broken |
| Quest | do the deed the company swore to | renown, and the deed's progress | the deed done and sung; done and hidden; failed honourably; abandoned |
| Caravan | carry goods to the great market at a profit | profit, and the caravan's name | rich; even; robbed; the contracts broken |
| Military campaign | carry out the campaign's orders | the campaign's objectives; the unit's strength | victory; a costly victory; retreat; mutiny |
| Embassy | win the host's favour, and bring back a treaty | favour with the host; secrets learned | treaty signed; an ally won; a war begun; recalled in disgrace |
| Raid | take plunder from the target and bring it home | the haul (coin and goods); renown | a great haul shared; a thin haul; beaten off; outlawed |
| Smuggling run | get the cargo past the law to the buyer | profit; heat (how watched you are) | paid; caught and fined; betrayed; the cargo dumped |
| Courier ride | deliver the message, whole, in time | time against par; the seal intact | on time and rewarded; late; intercepted; never arrived |
| Expedition | survey the unknown country and come back | knowledge: places and things recorded | a famous account; a thin report; lost men; turned back |
| Refugee flight | bring your people to safety together | people kept together; hope | a new home; scattered; turned back at the border |
| Treasure hunt | find the treasure | clues, toward the find | found and kept; found and sold; found and given back; a fool's errand |
| Monster hunt | kill or drive off the beast | signs of the beast; the trophy | the trophy; the beast driven off; the hunter hunted |
| Exile's flight | reach sanctuary ahead of pursuit | distance from pursuit; allies | sanctuary; a new name; taken |
| Royal progress | show the crown to its towns and keep them loyal | the towns' loyalty; the court's favour | a loyal realm; a plot uncovered; a town in revolt |
| Airship voyage | complete the circuit at a profit | profit; the ship's state | a profitable circuit; a wreck survived; lost |
| Arcane errand | gather what the working needs, and perform it | components and knowledge | the working done; a price paid for it; forbidden and fled |
| Mercenary contract | fight the contract and be paid | pay owed; the company's strength | paid in full; unpaid; broken by the fighting |
| Wandering | see the world | places seen and remembered | none: the road goes on |

## 5. Leg roles: the shared grammar

Under the names, every leg role is one of nine kinds. The kind is what the code runs; the name is what
the arc calls it.

| Kind | Objective shown | Completes when | Moves | Carried by | Fed by hooks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gather** | "Meet [person] at [place]." | you talk to them at the stop; the company forms | the company | the person; companions | the start town, its temple or market |
| **Purpose on the road** (harry, trade, trials, the march) | "On the way: [what the company does]." | you arrive; moments fire along the leg | the measure, moment by moment | towns, shrines, markets and people near the route | towns and markers within reach of the leg; price gaps |
| **Seek** | "Find someone in [place] who knows [what]." | you talk to the right person there | clues; knowledge | elder, innkeeper, merchant, captain, watch, shrine-keeper | ruins, wonders, faith seats, capitals, libraries |
| **Cross** | "Get past [border or zone]." | you are through | risk, heat | the watch, a toll post, a priest | borders of state, culture and faith; zones; regiments |
| **Endure** | "Reach [place] with the company whole." | you arrive with them | supplies, morale | wainwright, harbourmaster, elder (as advice) | hard ground, cold, heat, storms, long empty country |
| **Mislead** | shown as a Seek: the player does not know | the lie comes out at the stop | time lost; a new clue | a person who lies, by attitude | great cities, capitals, rivals |
| **Strike** | "Do [the deed] at [target]." | the deed, with the company present | the measure's great step | the company; the target's watch | walls, temples, monsters, ruins, the target town |
| **Escape** | "Get clear of [pursuit]." | distance, or the next stop | heat, safety | the watch; pursuers | the target's state; regiments |
| **Settle** | "[Share out / report / give thanks] at [place]." | the act at the stop | turns the measure into rewards | companions, patron, temple | the last town |

A leg role spans the leg and its arrival stop. Its objective points at the next stop; its moments
happen on the road; it completes at the stop, through a person or an act.

**Fitting the legs.** A journey of N stops has N − 1 legs.

1. **The target.** The climax happens at the target: the stop the journey's name gives, or the one
   the map marks (for raids and battles a hostile, walled or rich town; for hunts and digs a monster's
   or a ruin's country), or the last stop. The leg into the target is its **approach**: the scout,
   the lair, the guardian. A stop after the target holds the **return**.
2. **One leg.** The opening at the start, the whole middle of the arc as moments on the one road, the
   climax at the end. *Mistress Dorg walks to Leg*: the road of trials is 1,560 miles of moments.
3. **Two or three legs.** The opening; one middle role on each leg before the approach, the
   strongest the map supports; the approach; the climax.
4. **Four legs or more.** The structure's order across the legs; roles that repeat (harry, the market
   run, the march, the unknown country) fill extra legs; a structure with a turn puts it two-thirds of
   the way to the target, where a mislead or a revelation does most.
5. **Which role on which leg** is chosen by the leg's hooks: a leg across a faith border takes the
   border of faiths; a leg past ruins takes trace the rumour or the guardian; a long sea leg takes the
   long row; a leg through rich villages takes harry the coast.

## 7. Moments: how the map shows itself

Hooks are causes; moments are what the player meets. Every moment has a carrier (a person, a place, a
price, an event) and touches a system.

| Hook | What the player meets |
|---|---|
| A faith border | the shrines change; an innkeeper or priest asks what you worship; alms cost a stranger more; the market shuts on a holy day; a pilgrim joins or leaves the road |
| A culture border | the language changes (prices higher where you have no words; Alpha 7 brings speech); food and names change; the elder offers a guide |
| A state border | a toll post; papers; a fee for the crossing; news from the other side |
| A war, a regiment | soldiers requisition food; roads closed; recruiters; arms and horses dear |
| Plague | quarantine at the gates; healers busy and dear; a companion falls ill |
| Famine | beggars at the road; thefts at night; food dear; requests to share |
| Walls, a capital | the watch's questions; the city's laws; a great market; rumours of court |
| A price gap | the merchant's tip; a caravan going the other way |
| Hard ground, cold, heat | the elder's advice on where to camp; the wainwright's warning; a guide for hire |
| A monster marker | townsfolk warnings; tracks; a bounty on the watch's board |
| A ruin, a wonder | the elder's story; the shrine-keeper's warning; a map for sale |
| Towns near a raid's route | villages the raiders want to strike (a placeholder scene until Alpha 7) |

## 8. Carriers: the people

Every town has people to talk to. Each helps with the systems (as the harbourmaster and the wainwright
already do) and carries the story when the arc needs them.

| Person | Helps with (now, or planned) | Carries the story |
|---|---|---|
| Innkeeper | a room, meals, the local dish; who is in town; where to sleep on the road ahead | asks to hear your story (quests, adventures); rumours; lodging for pilgrims of one faith and not another |
| Merchant | the field: what is cheap here and dear elsewhere *(Alpha 4.1)* | the market run; the fence; the buyer; the double-cross |
| Shrine-keeper | alms, healing, blessing (faith standing), sanctuary *(planned: 5.x)* | the vigil; received at the seat; the mission; a relic's keeper; refusal to a stranger faith |
| Elder | the land: which ground feeds a camp and which starves it; local feuds; the place of minorities; directions in the wilds *(planned: 5.x)* | trace the rumour; old stories of ruins; the guide |
| Captain of the watch | the law: what is punished here, road safety, bounties; later papers and arrests *(planned: 5.x, Alpha 7)* | the inspection; the bounty; protection, or the lack of it |
| Townsfolk | rumours, work, directions | witnesses (mystery); villagers to harry; refugees on the road |
| Harbourmaster | directions, sailing, a watch on your vessel *(Alpha 4.1)* | the long row; the fleet's news; a ship to steal |
| Wainwright | the road, carts, horses *(Alpha 4.1)* | the lame horse; the fast road |
| Warehouse keeper | storage *(Alpha 3)*; a ledger *(Alpha 7)* | the switch; the cargo dumped |
| Shopkeeper | the town's food for strangers, and its kind | famine; requisition |
| Ship captains, passengers | passages; news from other ports | a passenger's secret; a rival aboard |
| Companions | their trades; the vote | the tie; their own small stakes; who stays at the end |
| Strangers on the road | travellers, pilgrims, refugees, raiders | joiners, rivals, pursuers |

**Asking about the journey.** Every person can be asked about it. What they know depends on who they
are (a captain knows the sea, a shrine-keeper the faith); whether they tell you depends on how they
see you (section 9). In a mystery this becomes questioning: each person holds a piece, and some lie.

## 9. Identity: who you are, and who they are

The game makes biomes real by camping: a rainforest feeds you, a swamp does not, and travellers learn to
avoid swamps. Identity works the same way. Every person you deal with has a disposition toward you,
built from simple parts.

| Part | Toward you |
|---|---|
| People | the same people +2; another −1; another that their state holds below its own −2 |
| Faith | the same faith +2; another −1; a faith at crusade with theirs −3 |
| Nationality | their state's stance to your home state: ally +2, friendly +1, neutral 0, suspicion −1, rival −2, enemy −3 |
| Their state's form | below |
| The world | war with your state −2; famine −1 toward strangers; plague −1 toward travellers |
| Their own day | hungry (their town's food short) −1 |
| Their nature | each person has one, seeded: generous +1, wary −1, devout (faith counts double), greedy (prices up, help for coin) |
| Your standing | your standing with their state and faith, as the game already keeps it |

**What it does.**

| Disposition | They |
|---|---|
| Warm (+3 or more) | sell a tenth cheaper, tell the truth, help, shelter you |
| Neutral | deal fairly |
| Cool (−2 to −1) | sell a tenth dearer, tell half of what they know |
| Hostile (−3 or less) | refuse you, report you, and at night someone may rob you |

**The state's form decides what the watch does about it.**

| Form (Bernoy has) | Another faith | Another people | Enemy nationals | The watch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Theocracy (4) | persecuted: −2 more, and no help from shrines | tolerated | arrested in war | enforces the faith; robbery of unbelievers goes unpunished |
| Monarchy (17) | tolerated if the crown is not devout | below the crown's people: −1 | detained in war | protects subjects; strangers at its pleasure |
| Republic (1) | protected | protected | watched | protects everyone who keeps the law |
| Union, Anarchy | as their parts | kin first | as their parts | thin |

**Bernoy.** A pilgrim of the Southan Deities who sets out from Shimberg, in the Duchy of Stilia
*(data)*, keeps a minority faith at home. In Zin, a kingdom of the Zizah Beliefs and Stilia's ally
*(data)*, the town is friendly to Stilians and cool to their faith. At South, the Ereachian Theocracy
of the Southan Deities *(data)*, the shrines welcome the faith, and the watch eyes a citizen of
Stilia, its rival *(data)*. The pilgrimage arrives home in its faith and abroad in its passport.

## 10. Three journeys, told

### The Leg Keels (Raid)

*(data)* Leg, in the Kingdom of Uzd, a walled port of 51,400 Uruk of the Old Uruk Beliefs → Anholdhol,
a port of the Scorc Theocracy (Dail, Greepeakism; neutral to Uzd), 5,296 miles by rowboat →
Vr'seramant, a walled port of the Kingdom of Zimorgerex (Rake, neutral), 1,823 miles → Negnud, a walled
port of the Duchy of Khuld: Uruk like the raiders, of the Uruk Religion, held in suspicion by Uzd.

*The story the data suggests:* the Keels sail against their own kin in Khuld, who left the old
beliefs. The long way round is for plunder and for cover.

> You are **Gukrul**, a raider of Leg, Uruk by birth and of the Old Uruk Beliefs. Your sister Mazog
> rows with the Keels, and you go with her. You stand in **Leg**, in a wet spring wind. The Keels are
> making for Anholdhol, taking what the Scorc fishing villages hold along the way. Mazog leads; you
> may row, or take the stores. **But first: wait out the tide and weather, and board the rowboat.**

| Leg | Role | Objective | What happens | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leg → Anholdhol | harry the coast | "On the way: the Keels strike the villages they pass." | the raiders call for each village near the route; a placeholder scene (Alpha 7 brings raids in detail); food runs short on a 5,296-mile row | the haul grows village by village |
| Anholdhol | the fence | "Find a merchant in Anholdhol who will take Scorc goods." | a theocracy of another faith: the shrines will not have you, the merchant will, at a price | goods become coin |
| Anholdhol → Vr'seramant | scout the target | "Learn Negnud's walls from the Rake, who trade with Khuld." | the innkeeper and merchant of Vr'seramant know Negnud; looking it over here is the point | odds at the strike |
| Negnud | the strike | "Strike Negnud, with the Keels." | needs the company; the town is ready if you were seen | the haul's great step |
| (after) | the share-out | "Share the haul." | each companion's cut; who stays | the haul becomes your fortune |

## 11. From the Alpha 5 playtest

| What happened | Why | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Only the first journey could be played | the title screen takes the map's first journey | a journey picker on the title card |
| The plan said horseback; the gold line went by sea | Bernoy's generated journeys send land legs over water: in *The Leg Keels*, 90% and 93% of the foot and horseback legs' lines lie on water *(data)*; so do legs of three other journeys | a leg that cannot go overland becomes "ride, then cross by sea": a passage stop is added, par is recomputed, and the story takes the crossing as a moment |
| The company rowboat could not be left or sold | the shipwright only deals with owned and hired vessels | the yard takes company vessels: leave her in the harbour's care, sell her, or hand her back to the company |
| "Ask the company": you had no company on a raid | the company forms only at a Gathering stop, and *The Leg Keels* has none | types with a company form it at the start; the opening names them |
| You could strike with your crew at the other side of the world | the climax checked nothing | a strike needs the company; without them the Keels strike without you, and the share is theirs |
| "Look the place over" at Vr'seramant, when the target was Negnud | the beat took the nearest walls as a trial | the leg into the target is its approach; scouting belongs there |
| Hook text read like data ("where the X gives way to the Y") | the scene showed the hook | moments replace hook text |
| The journey read as a list of binary choices | each beat was one scene | leg roles give objectives that last a leg; choices come from people and situations along it |
